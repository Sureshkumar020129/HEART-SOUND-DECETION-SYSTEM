System Architecture

How the Pipeline Operates

Heart sound auscultation captures soundwaves produced by the mechanical opening and closure of cardiac valves (primarily mitral, tricuspid, aortic, and pulmonary) along with turbulent blood acceleration. Here is how our computational pipeline converts raw acoustic recordings into auditable clinical screening indicators.

1
Audio Ingestion & Validation

Files uploaded in WAV, FLAC, MP3, or AIFF format (or captured via the browser Web Audio API) are validated with soundfile. The pipeline enforces minimum durations (1.0s) and checks for corrupted headers or silent files.

2
Preprocessing & Normalization

Signals are converted to single-channel mono, resampled with high-fidelity interpolation to 22.05 kHz, zero-padded or trimmed to a standardized 5.0s window, and normalized to unit peak amplitude to prevent loudness bias.

3
Feature Extraction Engine

Using librosa, we compute 40 Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (mean and standard deviation vectors = 80 features), 12 Chroma features, 7 Spectral Contrast bands, Spectral Centroid and Bandwidth, Zero Crossing Rate, and Mel energies.

4
ML Inference & Scaler Transform

The concatenated feature vector is transformed via a fitted StandardScaler and evaluated with an ensemble RandomForestClassifier. The model outputs class probabilities (Normal vs. Abnormal) and records confidence metrics.

5
Spectral & Waveform Visuals

Matplotlib renders headless base64 spectrogram and time-domain waveform visual plots directly into the database record for visual verification of murmur intervals and valve clicks.

6
PDF Reporting & Auditing

A formal clinical screening document is generated on demand via ReportLab, incorporating metadata, processing duration, probability distribution, and statutory medical disclaimers.
